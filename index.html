Dear Ma'am Sarah,

We've been trying to figure out how to put this into words — which is a little funny, considering you're the one who taught us how. So please forgive the run-on sentences and the occasional misplaced comma. We're still learning, and that's exactly why we're writing to you.

You taught us English, but it never felt like just another subject. You made stories feel alive, and you made essays feel less like a punishment and more like a place where our thoughts were allowed to exist. Even now, when we sit down to write, a little of your voice is there with us: "Read it again — does that sentence really say what you mean?"

And let's be honest: we struggled. We struggled with boundaries and we struggled with grammar — with knowing where one sentence should end and the next should begin. Our tenses wandered off on their own, our apostrophes landed wherever they pleased, and some of our paragraphs went on so long they practically needed a map. (this one might too…) You never once made us feel small for it. You just picked up your pen, circled the mistake, and showed us the way back — again, and again, and again.

You helped us with our essays far beyond what anyone would expect. You read our messy drafts, found the one good idea buried under everything else, and helped us shape it into something we were actually proud of.

But what we'll remember most isn't the grammar. It's that you talked to us about our problems. You listened — really listened — when we needed someone to. Some days we walked into your class carrying a lot more than our books, and somehow, you always noticed.

You made jokes that had the whole class laughing (and a few that only you laughed at — we saw that). You dealt with our shenanigans, our noise, our endless excuses and our "just five more minutes, ma'am" with a patience we honestly don't think we would have had.

You gave us respect, even when we were young and loud and didn't always earn it — and that made us want to give it right back. You were sweet in a way that is rare. Not once did you say anything to dishearten us. On the days we doubted ourselves, you were the one who didn't.

And Ma'am — if we ever upset you, we are truly sorry. For the times we were too loud, the homework that arrived "a little late", the moments we didn't listen when we should have, and anything we said or did that hurt you, even without meaning to. You never held it against us, and we hope you know it was never because we didn't care. We did. We still do.

So this little website is our token of appreciation. It isn't much — just some words, some fireworks, and a lot of love — but every single word of it is true.

With all our love and respect,Class 26B4
